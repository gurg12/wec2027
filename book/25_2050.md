# 2050 — The Audit

*Saskatoon, Saskatchewan. July 1, 2050.*

Tomek turns forty-four on Canada Day, and Amrita's gift is a bound facsimile of volume one — the 2026 notebook, the war and the gold price and the calorie count, his mother's bullion anxiety in the margins. Zofia, ten, reads it like archaeology: *"Tata, why did you write down the price of oil every day?"* Because, he tells her, everyone was afraid, and nobody could say of what, exactly, so we counted what we could. This chapter is the counting's reckoning: 2050 was always the year the century's forecasts named, so this book stops here — mid-book by calendar, end-of-argument by design — and audits the four great fears of its opening year against what the net actually held.

---

**FEAR ONE: The jobs apocalypse.** *Verdict: wrong shape.* **[The audit's confidence: HIGH — this is measurement now, not forecast.]** Unemployment in the rich world averaged 4.6% across 2026–2050; there was never a jobless decade. What there was: the missing rung (2027), the displaced articulate class, fifteen years of wage-insurance claims averaging 2–3 million a year in the US alone, the four-day norm, the purpose economy at 16% of employment, and the under-necessity crisis that arrived where the unemployment was supposed to be. Displacement was real and continuous; *unemployment* was the wrong instrument to watch. The costs landed in the meaning ledger, not the payroll ledger — and the 2041–42 renegotiations (transition insurance, third places, healthspan re-entry) were the actual policy response to the actual problem, a decade after the UBI debates aimed at the wrong one.¹

**FEAR TWO: The climate apocalypse.** *Verdict: halved, paid for, unevenly.* At +2.05°C, 2050 sits inside the corridor every 2020s scientist drew and below the tail every 2020s reader feared: emissions peaked 2035, down 22% since; the 4°C futures are dead, the 1.5°C future equally so; and the century tracks toward 2.2–2.4. The apocalypse was not averted so much as *amortized* — adaptation professionalized (11M practitioners), retreat proceduralized, Sail-class programs shaving the deadliest weeks off three regions' calendars — and the bill was paid regressively: the Indus baselines against the Chiang Rai drills, mortality bought down where balance sheets lived. Climate became what poverty already was: not an event but a gradient, governed, unjust, and survivable for most — which is both the century's achievement and its standing indictment.²

**FEAR THREE: The AI takeover.** *Verdict: category error.* No takeover, no treacherous turn, no paperclips. The risk that materialized was the one the 2026 skeptics and doomers were, in their different dialects, both pointing at: **dependence** — a civilization that routed its cognition through infrastructure it could neither see (the bifurcation's price) nor fully audit (Brazil's lesson) nor turn off (everything's lesson). The June Event stands as the era's Chernobyl: ~$310bn, low hundreds dead, no villain ever named — catastrophic *system* failure without catastrophic *intent*, which the 2020s' risk taxonomies mostly filed under "mundane." The response — accords, attestation, answerability chains — governed the dependence without ending it; the ICA audits 96% of frontier compute and no one audits the sum of a billion routine delegations. The author's honest 2050 sentence: *we do not know how much of our judgment we have outsourced, because outsourced judgment is precisely what stops keeping that ledger.*³ **[The open question, left open]**

**FEAR FOUR: The war.** *Verdict: contained — at Hormuz, at the strait, in Brazil's aftermath, on the ceasefire line — by deterrence, dependence, exhaustion, and an unpriced quantity of luck.* The Long Peace turned 105 this year. The violence the era did produce — the Sahel's decade, the Indus displacements, the drone-and-sanction sieges — killed by the hundreds of thousands, not the hundreds of millions, and the audit refuses the comfort of that comparison even as it records it. The 2026 dossier's own base-rate note stands as the century's epistemic epitaph: *the streak is statistically surprising, and the deterrence explanation is contested.* We got the median path. The tails did not vanish because we didn't draw them.⁴
