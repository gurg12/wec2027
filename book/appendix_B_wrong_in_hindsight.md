# Appendix B — Wrong in Hindsight: The Five Pre-Registered Failure Modes

Written July 2026, before any chapter could flatter itself. These are the five most likely ways this document fails — ranked by the author's estimate of (probability × how much of the book each would invalidate). A forecast that cannot name its own failure modes is advertising.

## 1. AI capability timing — in either direction (the largest single risk)

The spine bets on *gradual and jagged*: capability compounding through crashes, reliability lagging benchmarks, integration friction eating each advance's punch for years, measured TFP arriving ~2030. Both tails are live. **Too fast:** if the recursive-improvement loop closes cleanly (Branch One's other road, ~30% at 2028 and nonzero every year after), the entire post-2029 architecture — bifurcation, accords, boring governance — is fantasy; the real 2030s get written by a race. **Too slow:** if the 2026 reliability wall is structural rather than frictional — if resident agents plateau as expensive autocomplete — then the productivity inflection (Ch. 5), the AWTA's funding base, cognition-too-cheap-to-meter, and the boundary settlement all slide a decade or dissolve. Everything in this book downstream of "the agents worked, eventually, boringly" — which is most of it — inherits this bet.

## 2. A Taiwan tail realized

The book carries a ~20% residual coercion risk past the 2038 compact and lets the median path collect the other 80%. If the tail fires — a quarantine that doesn't end on day nine, a leadership discontinuity in Beijing that makes the symbolic urgent again — the order string breaks at its load-bearing knot: blocs harden, the accords likely die in the mobilization, the swing bloc is forced to choose, and the century's "violence stayed civil, cyber, and climatic" verdict is revoked. No other single event in the scenario space converts this book from roughly-right to structurally-wrong as completely. The 2028 drill chapter was written, deliberately, as the reader's felt preview of the stakes.

## 3. A pandemic-class biological event — deliberately omitted

The spine contains no pandemic. Not because the risk is low — base rates alone (three global respiratory events in the last century, plus engineered-pathogen risk rising with exactly the biotech curve this book celebrates) make ~30 pandemic-free years perhaps a coin flip — but because timing is unforecastable and a scenario must choose: salt the timeline with an arbitrary plague-year or pre-register the omission. This book chose omission, and here is the receipt. If it happens, every ledger series bends at that year, the biology-turn chapters read as bitter irony, and the reader is asked to grade the *strings* (which should survive: the same diagnostics, provenance, and adaptation infrastructure this book builds are the response toolkit) rather than the calendar.

## 4. Fertility collapsing even faster than assumed

This book already bets below consensus (peak ~9.3bn in the mid-2040s vs the UN's 2084). The nightmare version — East Asian floors (0.7–0.9) becoming the global urban default within a generation, the Sweden–Korea upticks proving compositional noise, Africa's transition running at the same leapfrog speed as its electrification — would pull the peak toward 2040 and ~9.1bn, break the immigration-arithmetic that stabilizes every aged-democracy chapter, and turn the "vertigo not catastrophe" verdict of 2044 into whistling past a graveyard. The meaning-recession and fertility strings are coupled; if the 2041 trough is not a trough but a floor-finding, Chapters 16, 19, 26, and 30's domestic warmth are the book's most embarrassing pages.

## 5. The meaning recession arrives earlier and eats the story

The spine schedules the psychological crisis as a 2041 peak, after the economic settlements land. The alternative ordering — under-necessity metastasizing in the early 2030s, before wage insurance, third places, and the medical thaw build any floor — produces a different decade: the backlash never thaws, the bifurcation reopens, anti-AI politics wins a major election outright, and the century's dominant story becomes anthropological rather than economic, with every institutional chapter in this book demoted to background. The author rates this the most *underpriced* failure mode in the set: the 2026 evidence for it (Gen Z sentiment collapsing while usage rose; the displaced articulate class) was already stronger than the evidence for several [MED] beats that made the spine.

If the reader of 2056 is grading this in a discontinuous world, the failure was not in any string but in the drawing hand. That, too, was pre-registered. — July 2026*
