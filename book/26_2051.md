# 2051 — The Gerontocracy Peak

*Lagos, Nigeria. February 9, 2051.*

Dele Adeyemi, forty-three, chairs Corridor Current from a boardroom whose median age is thirty-one, and spends this morning on a video call with a Tokyo counterpart whose *junior* negotiating team's median age is sixty-eight. Neither man remarks on it anymore. The corridor sells the aged world energy, fuels, logistics — and, increasingly, the only commodity that cannot be synthesized: workforce. His daughter, twelve, asks at dinner why the news calls Japan's election "the last grandparents' election." He shows her the pyramid charts, his and theirs, base and inversion. "Because over there," he says, "the young are so few they've become precious, and the old are so many they've become the government. Here it's the opposite, which is why they call us the future and pay us like it. Neither is fair, Ada. Both are arithmetic."

---

**In April**, the aged democracies' arithmetic peaks and, for the first time, gets formally contested. Median voter age crosses sixty in Japan and Italy this cycle, fifty-six across the EU; pensioners out-vote under-thirties nearly three to one where turnout gradients bite — and the year's policy record reads accordingly, healthspan indexation notwithstanding: housing, debt, and climate burdens tilted, budget by budget, onto the scarce young.¹ **[MED]** The counter-move the spine promised arrives on schedule: **youth-weighting** enters serious politics — Demeny voting (parents casting proxies for children) passes first reading in the Diet before dying in committee; a Portuguese commission proposes ballot weights indexed to remaining life expectancy, is denounced by everyone, and polls at 41%; and the scarce, organized young — the missing-rung generation's children, priced like the assets they are — extract instead the settlement that actually passes everywhere: *fiscal* youth-weighting, generational-impact scoring attached to every budget line, enforced by constitutional courts in four countries by decade's end.² **[LOW on electoral reform passing; MED on the fiscal substitute]** Nothing so tidy as justice; procedure again, arriving late, doing most of the work — the young do not get extra ballots, they get *standing*, and the gerontocracy peak enters the record books the way this book's peaks do: visible only in the rearview, dated retroactively to about this year, eroded thereafter by the same longevity that built it, as treated eighty-year-olds prove reliably unwilling to vote like a bloc whose members plan to be around for the consequences.³
