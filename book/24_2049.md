# 2049 — The Long Normalization

*Novosibirsk, Russia. March 12, 2049.*

Sergei Dorokhov, fifty-one, runs the intermodal yard where the Chinese corridor meets the reopened European lines, and his badge lanyard carries, in the manner of his generation, no flags at all. He drove a supply truck at Kupiansk in '25; his brother did not come back from earlier and worse. What he governs now — deputy chair of the oblast's trade council, the war generation's standard second act — is throughput: grain east, machine tools west, attested compute transit under ICA seal, the tariffed, inspected, unloved commerce of a country that has become what the analysts said it would and none of its poets wanted: *an authoritarian-lite trading state, reopened, load-bearing, beside the point.* At the yard's twentieth-anniversary lunch a Moscow journalist asks what the war generation believes in. Sergei gives her the survivors' catechism, refined over two decades of such lunches: "Schedules. Warranty clauses. That the man across the table also buried someone and also prefers this." She asks if that is cynicism. "It is the *opposite*," he says, standing to check a manifest. "Cynicism got the boys killed. This" — he gestures at the yard, the cranes, the queue of trucks from four countries — "is the sincerity we could afford."

---

**In June**, the first act closes on schedule: the Kolesnikov succession — the caretaker's own, managed this time without even a medical bulletin — completes the normalization's institutionalization: a security-services selectorate that has now transferred power twice without a body, sanctions down to a residual attestation-and-arms list, EU trade at 60% of pre-war and rising, and the ceasefire line's twenty-second November renewal passing without debate in either parliament.¹ **[MED]** What Russia did not become is the ledger's other column: not democratic, not confessed, not trusted — the war's chroniclers still prosecuted faster than its profiteers, the exiles' archive in Vilnius still the true national library, Ukraine's EU membership (acceded 2038, thriving) still the standing counterexample one border away that Moscow's textbooks route around. Normalization, this book's oldest pattern insists, is not redemption; it is *procedure with a pension plan* — and the war generation that governs it, Sergei's generation, governs precisely as the spine promised: with the specific cynicism of survivors, which is to say, with care.²

**In October**, Tomek Weber-Okonkwo — forty-three, now the province's deputy minister for grid and water, the notebook a professional archive his staff cites in briefings — hosts his counterpart from the Omsk region for the Arctic-corridor water talks, and finds across the table a Kupiansk veteran with warranty-clause sincerity and a granddaughter Zofia's age. The agreement they initial is small: flow data, flood-pulse coordination, a joint monitoring station. The notebook's entry is smaller and larger: *Signed water data with Russia today. Twenty years ago I tracked their war on my phone at lunch. Neither of us mentioned it. Both of us knew the other knew. That, apparently, is what peace is: the thing you both don't say, notarized.*³
